## 🩺 Most relevant to practice

**[End-to-End Clinical Validation of a Human-Supervised Large Language Model Agent for Enterprise Surgical Pathology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42526601/)**
*Modern Pathology*
An LLM agent taken through actual clinical validation with a human in the loop, rather than
benchmarked offline. The **validation design** is the interesting part regardless of what one
thinks of the tool — see [Validation, QA & Regulatory Science](../browse/category/validation-regulatory.md).

**[Consensus-Based Minimum Requirements for the Adoption of a Computational Pathology Algorithm for Tumor Budding](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42536993/)**
*JCO Clinical Cancer Informatics*
A consensus statement on what a laboratory should require *before* adopting an algorithm.
This is the genre of paper the field has least of and needs most.

**[Multiclass diagnostic performance and error patterns of a multimodal large language model in oral histopathology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42531661/)**
*Journal of Oral Biosciences*
Notable for reporting **error patterns**, not just accuracy. How a model fails matters more
than how often — see [Ethics, Bias & Failure Modes](../browse/category/ethics-safety.md).

**[The role of artificial intelligence in thyroid cytology of indeterminate nodules](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42523677/)**
*Frontiers in Endocrinology*
Cytopathology remains thinly covered by AI work relative to its diagnostic burden.

## 🔬 Methods and critique

**[Rethinking pathology image analysis through shuffling](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42527448/)**
*npj Biomedical Innovations*
A critique paper. Worth reading alongside the
[site-signature result](../browse/category/ethics-safety.md) — this field's most useful
papers are often the ones testing whether a result means what it appears to.

**[Screening efficiency over experience: rapid target detection in the low-power field](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42516004/)**
*Cancer Cytopathology*
Human factors rather than algorithms: how pathologists actually search a slide. Relevant to
anyone designing a tool meant to fit into that behaviour.

**[Methodological Considerations on Central Pathology Review in AI-Assisted Gastric Neoplasia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42532158/)** · [Author reply](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42532160/)
*Gastroenterology*
A published methodological disagreement, with the reply. Reading both is more instructive
than reading either.

**[Object detection in histology: a multi-dataset benchmark and test-time inference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42525693/)**
*PLOS ONE* — multi-dataset, which is the part that matters.

**[KongNet: a multi-headed model for nuclei detection and classification](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42526077/)**
*Medical Image Analysis* — compare against
[existing nucleus models](../browse/task/nuclei-segmentation.md).

**[Risk stratification of IDH-wildtype glioblastoma from histopathology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42520553/)**
*European Journal of Surgical Oncology*

## 📌 Not included, and why

coursework repositories from GitHub's recently-created feed — real work, but not yet

something notable is missing, [say so](https://github.com/atultiwari/awesome-ai-pathology/issues/new/choose)
